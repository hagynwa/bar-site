# Safer devices, speakers and the legal side

Research for the "עוד קצת ילדות" campaign (a parents' committee at an elementary school in Tzofim asking families to delay a smartphone until the end of 5th grade, ending in a signed community pledge and a signing ceremony).

Compiled: 19 November 2026. I looked up every source below while writing this. Prices marked **live** were read from the retailer's or Zap's own page on 19 Nov 2026. KSP, Ivory, Office Depot, Payngo's live pages and the Ministry of Education circulars site blocked automated access from where this research ran, so their prices are not given here or come only from search-engine snapshots, and I say so. **"Unverified"** means I could not confirm the point from a primary or dated source. This is research, not legal advice: have a lawyer or accountant check the legal points before the committee acts on them.

### B. Recommended purchase model

**A "recommended devices" page on the campaign site, plus a negotiated coupon code with one or two Israeli retailers or the importer. Each family buys directly from the retailer.** Under this model:

- The committee **never takes money, holds stock or delivers devices**. The retailer sells to each family, issues the receipt, and handles the warranty and the 14-day cancellation right.
- The committee takes **no commission**, or at most passes the full discount to families. If an affiliate link is ever used, say so openly on the page.
- If enough families sign up for a **collective order**, the supplier should open a dedicated order page or link, invoice each family directly, and deliver to homes or to a non-school pickup point.
- **Nothing is marketed through the school:** no flyers in pupils' bags, no retailer at a signing ceremony held on school grounds, no school logo next to a retailer's, and no sharing of the signers list with any retailer. Show the device page to the principal before going live.
- Precedent: in Jerusalem, the Yehuda HaLevi school **bought basic phones for every 3rd grader** (Ynet, June 2026), so a school-led purchase is possible. That was the school acting, though, not a parents' committee selling devices.

### D. Key legal do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Keep the device page on the **campaign site** (not the school's site), framed as information for parents. | Don't market, advertise or hand out products **in the school, to pupils, or via pupils**. The 2007 Commercial Activity in Educational Institutions Law and Ministry of Education standing directive 0035 prohibit this without a written permit from the Ministry's committee. |
| Let the **retailer be the seller**: its receipt, its warranty, its 14-day cancellation terms. | Don't have the committee collect money and resell. That makes it the "dealer" under consumer and VAT law, which a volunteer committee is not set up to be. |
| Coordinate publicity with the **principal** (school parents'-leadership bylaws require this). | Don't give any retailer the pledge list or parents' contact details. The 2007 law also bars staff from passing pupil or family details to a business. |
| On the pledge form, give the **Privacy Law section 11 notice**: that giving details is voluntary, the purpose, who will see them, and the rights to view and correct them. Ask for **separate opt-in consent** to appear on the public list. | Don't publish **children's names**, classes or phone numbers. Don't collect ID numbers, addresses or a child's details beyond the grade. |
| Collect the **minimum**: parent name, child's grade, one contact (email *or* phone), consent boxes. Set a **deletion date** (for example, the end of 5th grade). | Don't send promotional messages (for example "10% off phones") by SMS or email without **prior explicit consent**. Israel's anti-spam rule in Communications Law §30A covers commercial messages. |
| Keep the data in an access-controlled tool (Google Workspace or Airtable with 2-factor login). Show the public list as "Name + initial" or "family name only". | Don't assume "small = exempt". Amendment 13 abolished most database registration, but the notice, purpose-limitation, security and data-subject rights duties still apply. |

## 1. Devices

### 1.0 What the authorities now recommend (useful for the campaign)

- **Health Ministry, September 2026** (reported by Arutz Sheva on 3 Sep 2026 and by Ynet; I did not reach the Ministry's own page):
  - no screens under age 2;
  - ages 2-5: up to 1 hour a day;
  - ages 6-9: under 2 hours of leisure screen time;
  - ages 10-12: up to 3 hours;
  - **a basic, non-smartphone phone at about 9-11**;
  - **a smartphone from about 13, at first without social-media apps**;
  - **social media from 15**, with parental guidance.
  - Named officials: Director-General Moshe Bar Siman Tov and Dr. Gilad Bodenheimer, head of the Mental Health Division. The guidelines drew on the Israel Pediatric Association.
  - Sources: https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/432659 and https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/bjdj6wpome
- **HBSC 2025** (Bar-Ilan and the Ministry of Education; Prof. Yossi Harel-Fisch): the share of pupils in grades 7-12 classed as addicted to social media rose from 4.1% in 2019 to **16.3% in 2025**, and 28.7% spend 6+ hours a day on screens outside school. https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/s1helnmpfe
- **Ministry of Education:** a ban on using phones anywhere on **elementary-school** grounds was announced on 11 Dec 2025, in force from February 2026 (phones may be brought but not used). It is planned to extend to middle schools in 2026-27. https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/article-880011 and https://www.jns.org/how-to-separate-elementary-school-kids-from-their-smartphones/

### 1.1 The 2G/3G shutdown and what it means for basic phones

- **Original plan:** 2G/3G networks off by 31 Dec 2025, with 2G/3G-only devices stopping from 1 Jan 2026. Imports of 2G/3G-only phones were banned in 2022. https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/hy6opdnexl
- **Revised outline (Ministry hearing, 4 Dec 2025):**
  - From 1 Jan 2026, operators *may* keep 2G/3G running at their own discretion.
  - The low 800/900 MHz bands had to be cleared for 4G/5G by **1 Feb 2026**.
  - Operators must keep a dedicated 2G network for **emergency calls (100/101/102) until the end of 2028**, because about 300,000 users have phones that make emergency calls only over 3G even though they support 4G and VoLTE.
  - https://www.gadgety.co.il/354218/2g-3g-israel-update/
- **Status, 10 Feb 2026:** the first band was cleared on 1 Feb 2026 without special faults. Old phones get "basic service in the near future" and emergency dialling until the end of 2028; reception is expected to get worse, and operators may switch the old networks off completely later. https://ch10.co.il/en/?p=1045440
- **Import and connection rules:** the Ministry proposed limiting commercial imports of phones that can't make emergency calls over VoLTE, and barring operators from connecting new devices of that kind (comments were due by 24 Feb 2026). **I could not confirm whether this was adopted.** https://www.gadgety.co.il/357367/emergency-phone-alerts-update/
- **What this means for families:**
  - Old 2G/3G basic phones (hand-me-downs, older "kosher" phones) will lose coverage quality and may stop working.
  - Buy a 4G model with VoLTE, sold by an Israeli importer or store, and check it on **\*235** (the Ministry's checking line).
  - Imported "global" phones (Light Phone, Punkt and others) may lack VoLTE support on a given Israeli operator, because each operator keeps its own list of VoLTE-supported devices. Example: we-com's July 2026 list names only mainstream smartphones. https://we-com.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/מכשירים-תומכי-VoLTE-V3.pdf
  - The Ministry said in 2025 that **20+ basic 4G/VoLTE models** were sold in Israel at **₪150-600** (as reported by Ynet). https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/hy6opdnexl

Stores sell the same Nokia models in "regular" and "kosher" versions; the regular version is what to link to.

### 2.2 Consumer-protection basics (if anyone in the chain sells online)

Consumer Protection Law, distance sales (§14C):

- The seller must give written disclosure of the deal's details.
- The buyer may cancel **within 14 days** of receiving the product or the disclosure document, whichever is later.
- The cancellation fee is capped at **5% of the price or ₪100, whichever is lower**.
- The refund is due within 14 days of the cancellation notice.
- Seniors (65+), people with disabilities and new immigrants get an extended **4 months** where the sale involved a phone call or chat (§14C1).

Sources: https://www.consumers.org.il/files/guide/guide3_h.docx and https://protocol.co.il/remote-selling-transaction-cancellation/

These duties fall on the **seller (עוסק)**, which is one more reason to let the retailer be the seller. The VAT and receipts point (whoever sells must be a registered dealer and issue a tax invoice) is the general rule; I did not research a parents'-committee-specific ruling.

### 2.3 Ministry of Education rules on commercial activity in schools

- **The law:** the **Commercial Activity in Educational Institutions Prohibition Law, 2007** (חוק איסור פעילות מסחרית במוסדות חינוך, התשס"ח-2007; Knesset 25 Dec 2007).
  - **§2:** a **principal shall not allow a business (עוסק) or advertiser** to do any of the following on school premises:
    1. enter during school hours for advertising, marketing, promotion or product display;
    2. display advertising in any way;
    3. distribute promotional material or gifts, hold raffles, or have pupils sign documents (during school hours);
    4. use teaching aids to advertise;
    5. **collect identifying details of pupils or their family members**.
  - **§3:** staff who learn pupil or family information through their work may not pass it to a business or advertiser.
  - **§4:** the Ministry's Director-General may grant exemptions.
  - https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_פעילות_מסחרית_במוסדות_חינוך and https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_פעילות_מסחרית_במוסדות_חינוך
- **The circular:** a Director-General circular on the general ban on commercial advertising in educational institutions, Ministry siduri 30 (https://apps.education.gov.il/Mankal/Horaa.aspx?siduri=30).
  - A secondary source dates it to August 2017 as standing directive **0035** (https://stairs-adv.co.il/commercial-advertising-in-schools/). **I could not open the Ministry page itself; it refused automated access.** Quotes below are taken from a summary that cites its sections (https://www.hiloni.org.il/איסור-פרסומת-מסחרית-במוסדות-החינוך):
  - **2.6:** any cooperation with an outside body, including a business, must go to the **committee for approving commercial advertising** (הוועדה לאישור שילוב פרסומת מסחרית).
  - **3.10:** the committee prohibits "חלוקת חומר פרסומי ושיווקי", "חלוקת מתנות ושוברים להגרלות", "קבלת פרטים מזהים מתלמידים", "החתמת תלמידים על מסמכים" and "מגע ישיר עם תלמידים".
  - **4.3:** staff who allow product distribution or commercial activity against Ministry guidance commit an offence under the Consumer Protection (Advertising and Marketing Aimed at Minors) Regulations, 1991, and face discipline.
  - **4.4:** the principal bears full responsibility.
- **Parents' committees:** the law and circular are addressed to principals and staff, not to parents' committees directly. **A committee using the school's channels** (pupils, school premises, the school's name or logo, official class lists) could still put the principal in breach. School parents'-leadership bylaws also require central-committee publicity to be coordinated with the principal. https://www.mamah.org.il/תקנון-הנהגת-הורים
- **Note on the pledge itself:** the circular's ban on "having pupils sign documents" concerns businesses. Still, **have parents sign, not children**, and keep any retailer away from the signing ceremony if it is held at school.

## 3. Speakers and organisations

### 3.1 Attention Revolution (מהפכת הקשב), the leading option

- **Who:** founded in 2021 by graduates and staff of the Mevo'ot (מבוע) beit midrash, inspired by Dr. Micah Goodman's book. It is a public-benefit company (חל"צ 516779097).
  - **Founder:** Asaf Granot, an educator and social entrepreneur. He founded the "בשביל" youth programme and is completing an MA in integrative psychotherapy at the University of Haifa.
  - **CEO:** Asaf Banner (בנר). JPost spells it "Banner"; the Kenes media piece spells it "Baner".
  - **Tel Aviv coordinator:** Yael Shmueli, a certified Adler Institute parent guide and former Meta operations manager.
  - https://keshev.online/about/
- **Scale:** 11 local authorities including Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, Nahariya, Gush Etzion and Mate Yehuda. Ynet (5 Jun 2026) says 120+ schools. JPost (22 Jul 2026) says 96 elementary and 49 secondary schools, about 77,500 pupils. The movement's own site says "dozens of communities".
- **What they offer:**
  - a two-wave survey of parents and staff (before and after);
  - a kickoff meeting with council reps, the principal, the parents' committee and volunteers;
  - a **community "exposure" lecture**;
  - workshops for parents and staff, and **lesson units for pupils** on attention, the brain and digital communication;
  - forming a pact. They aim for **70-80% agreement**, counting "willing to try" answers;
  - implementation support.
  - **Lectures** include "העסקה הנסתרת", "איזון דיגיטלי" and "המלכוד הדיגיטלי".
  - **"Activities are recognised in the Ministry of Education's GEFEN programme."**
  - https://keshev.online/schools-and-communities/ and https://keshev.online/lectures-and-workshops/
- **Contact:** info@keshev.online, 055-2971990. Fees are not published.

## 5. Privacy: Amendment 13 and the pledge website

Main source: the Ministry of Justice / Privacy Protection Authority guide to Amendment 13 (May 2025). https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/guide_tikon13_professional/he/tikun%2013%20_170825.pdf

The amendment was passed on 5 Aug 2024 and is **in force since 14 Aug 2025**.

1. **Does the law apply to a volunteer committee?**
   - The database provisions don't apply to a collection kept for personal use "שאינו למטרות עסק או למטרות ציבוריות". A community campaign is plausibly a **public purpose**, so assume the law applies.
   - Amendment 13 defines "personal information" broadly as *any* data about an identifiable person.
2. **Is it a "database"?**
   - A collection containing **only name, address and contact details** of 100,000 people or fewer, which does not in itself reveal further personal information, is *excluded* from the definition.
   - A pledge list also shows the child's **grade** and the family's **stance on smartphones**, so it reveals more. **Treat it as a database.**
3. **Registration:**
   - For private bodies, registration has been "almost entirely abolished". It remains for (a) databases of **public bodies** and (b) data-trading or direct-mail databases with 10,000+ people.
   - A parents' committee's pledge list needs **no registration**.
   - **Caveat:** if the school or the local council (both public bodies) runs the list, registration rules for public bodies may apply. Keep it with the committee or campaign.
   - The new "notice to the Authority" duty applies only to databases with especially sensitive information on 100,000+ people, so it is not relevant here.
4. **The notice duty (§11), expanded by Amendment 13.** Every request for personal information must state:
   - whether giving it is a legal duty or voluntary, and **what happens if you decline**;
   - the **purpose**;
   - **who will receive it** and why;
   - the **right to view** the information (§13);
   - the **right to request correction** (§14).
   - Missing notice can draw an administrative fine of ₪50 per person approached, with a ₪30,000 minimum per breach, and can be sued for **without proof of damage, up to ₪10,000**.
   - Put a short Hebrew notice right on the form. Example: "מסירת הפרטים היא מרצון; ללא שם וכיתה לא נוכל לרשום את החתימה. הפרטים ישמשו את ועד ההורים לניהול האמנה בלבד, לא יועברו לאף גורם מסחרי, ויימחקו בסוף שנת הלימודים של כיתה ה'. ניתן לעיין בפרטים ולבקש לתקנם או למחקם ב-[email]".
5. **Purpose limitation (§8(b)) and no unauthorised processing (§8(c)):**
   - Use the data only for the pledge.
   - Don't reuse the list for unrelated mailings or for the device purchase.
   - Only named committee members get access.
6. **Security:** the Information Security Regulations (2017) still apply; the guide confirms this even for unregistered databases. In practice:
   - a written "database definitions" note (מסמך הגדרות מאגר) of one page: what, why, who has access, where it is stored;
   - access-controlled storage with 2-factor login;
   - no copies in WhatsApp groups;
   - delete the data at the set date.
7. **The public list of signers.** Amendment 13 does not specifically address publishing a list, so this is the general privacy principle plus good practice:
   - Publish only with **separate, explicit opt-in** ("אני מסכים/ה ששמי יופיע ברשימת החותמים באתר").
   - Show **parents' names only**, for example "משפחת כהן" or "דנה כ.". Show **no children's names**, no class letter and no contact details.
   - Let families sign without appearing ("+12 families who prefer not to be listed").
   - Remove anyone who asks, promptly.
   - Remember that a list of who did *not* sign can create social pressure. Don't publish or circulate it.
8. **Consent for messages:** campaign updates to signers are fine within the stated purpose. **Commercial messages** (discount codes, retailer offers) by SMS or email need prior explicit consent under the anti-spam provision (Communications Law §30A). Use a separate unticked checkbox. (This is a well-known rule; I did not re-read the statute for this report.)
9. **Minimise:** don't collect ID numbers, home addresses, the child's full name or date of birth. Name, grade and one contact method are enough.
